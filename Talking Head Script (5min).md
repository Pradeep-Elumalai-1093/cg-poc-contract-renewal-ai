# Proactive Contract Renewal — Talking Head Script
**Target runtime: ~5:00** (presenter to camera, cutting to screen where noted)

---

**[0:00–0:35] — To camera**

Every year, Carrier's Climate Solutions business renews thousands of HVAC service contracts across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific. Every day, in the background, the system scans that entire book and automatically generates fresh renewal recommendations for the accounts that need them. ~~That part runs on its own — what I want to show you is where those recommendations actually land, and what a rep does with them.~~

**[0:35–1:17] — Screen: Renewal Prioritization tab**

This is the Renewal Prioritization screen — an account manager's home base. Contracts are bucketed by days to expiry, and filterable by region and channel. And this chart right here is the heart of it: every contract, plotted by risk score against contract value. The ones up here, in the top right — high risk *and* high value — those are the accounts that matter most. And if you need a closer look, the whole chart is zoomable — you can pop it open full-screen and dig into a cluster.

**[1:17–2:02] — Screen: filters, scatter chart, KPI cards, campaign response chart**

Everything on this screen is live. Change the region filter, change the channel, click a different expiry bucket — and the scatter chart, the portfolio KPI cards, all of it updates instantly. Those KPIs cover things like revenue at risk, converted value, and campaign response rate, so you get an immediate read on portfolio health for whatever slice you're looking at. And right alongside it is a campaign-response chart broken out by risk bucket, so you can see at a glance whether outreach is actually landing better with high-risk accounts or lower-risk ones.

**[2:02–2:15] — Screen: scroll to worklist**

Scroll down, and you hit the worklist. It's fully sortable — by risk, by value, by region — so a rep can just work straight down the list in priority order.

**[2:15–2:32] — Screen: worklist detail**

And it's built around the real relationship, not just a flat contract list: one customer can have several contracts, and you can filter down to exactly the group you care about using the contract-expiring buckets up top.

**[2:32–3:23] — Screen: contract detail panel opens**

Click on a customer or a contract, and a panel opens with the recommendation right up front. This is where the AI has actually done the work — it's already read the service history, the payment pattern, the sentiment in past feedback — and it hands you a plain-language summary of the whole relationship. Then it recommends one specific campaign action, with the reasoning behind it, and drafts the actual outreach email, ready to go. And every one of those recommendations gets a second AI review before a human ever sees it, so anything shaky gets flagged for a person to check instead of just being sent out.

**[3:23–3:36] — Screen: Send email**

~~When you're happy with it,~~ hitting send opens Outlook with the email already drafted — subject, body, everything. No copying, no pasting — the rep just reviews it and sends.

**[3:36–4:05] — Screen: Log outcome**

Once the rep reaches out, they log what happened — engaged, declined, or no response. And if it's a no response, that doesn't just disappear. It feeds forward as context for the next milestone, so when that contract comes up again at 60 days, or 45 days, the AI already knows what was tried — and recommends something different, instead of repeating itself.

**[4:05–4:38] — Screen: Dashboard tab, campaign performance**

Zoom out to the Dashboard, and you get the same picture rolled up globally and by region — North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific. There's a full campaign performance view too: for each type of campaign, you can see how many accounts were assigned, engaged, declined, or gave no response, and what that means in real dollars — total contract value, revenue at risk, and what's actually been converted.

**[4:38–4:50] — Screen: Trace & Agent Metrics**

And for anyone who wants to audit the AI itself, every recommendation is fully traceable — the prompts, the scores, the retries, right down to the cost.

**[4:50–4:58] — To camera**

One engine, three regions. From spotting the risk, to recommending the action, to proving what actually worked.
